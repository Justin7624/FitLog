# FitLog 5.0 — 360° Body Scan (experimental)

## Upgrade without losing existing records

1. Open your current FitLog website and export its existing full backup, including progress photos. Keep it off GitHub.
2. Extract the ZIP. Replace the files in the **root of your existing FitLog repository** (same GitHub Pages publishing directory). All runtime JS/CSS, including `body-mesh.js`, are at the root; no nested asset directory is needed. Preserve the `tests/`, `tools/`, and `.github/workflows/` directory structure if uploading development files. Do not upload personal backups, photos, or exports.
3. Wait for Pages to finish its deployment, then refresh. Look for `360° BODY SCAN · 5.0` in the header. Body → **Open 360° Body Scan**. The earlier measurement-only Body Studio is still available.
4. If an older header remains, hard-refresh after the deployment. If necessary use Chrome DevTools → Application → Service Workers → Unregister, then reload. **Do not clear site data, local storage, or IndexedDB.** The site URL, origin, and browser profile must stay the same.

The v1/v2/v3/v4 workout keys, progress-photo DB, and data schema are retained. No real user records are in the source package. The new scanner stores its records separately, so an older FitLog backup does not contain scans. Existing cloud synchronization does not sync scans.

## Capture instructions

- Use an adult subject who consents; fitted opaque clothing, no shoes, even illumination and a contrasting, plain wall without furniture or shadows. Avoid mirror images or mirrored selfie frames.
- Set a stationary camera around waist height, level, far enough away to reduce perspective. Keep the entire body including feet and head visible, a margin around it, the same distance and position, arms slightly away from the sides and feet apart. Keep posture unchanged as you turn left by 30° per shot. The camera sees your right side at 90°, back at 180° and left side at 270°. The 0° frame faces the camera. The template has a fixed A-like pose.
- Use the guided timer (3 seconds per selected angle or 8 seconds per angle for the full sequence), or upload twelve files named in angle order (00–11). The optional voice guidance speaks only angle instructions. Camera permission and HTTPS are required. No actual turn/pose detection is performed.
- Alternatively import a video of one evenly paced 360° turn, specify its start/end, and sample twelve evenly spaced frames. Verify each frame's actual angle. No automatic pose-based selection is included. Browser-supported video/image formats only.
- Review every mask. Green marks foreground; background is segmented by color distance from border median or alpha cutout. Adjust threshold, paint to add/remove, or retake. Transparent PNG cutouts can be uploaded. Check for missing limbs, clothing and shadows. Approve each usable mask. Framing and foreground area checks are mandatory; brightness/edge contrast are advisory, not a validated blur detector. Dashed landmarks are silhouette envelope guides, not AI joint detections.
- At least 8 valid, distinct angles with no angular gap larger than 90° are required. All 12 are preferred. Height in cm is required to establish scale. Existing journal inputs are prefilled; verify the date, shoulder width vs circumference and units. Changes in the scan form do not overwrite the journal. Weight/fat are optional priors, not scan-derived measurements.

## What the algorithm actually does

The original CC0 continuous template (21,756 vertices, 43,508 triangles) is fitted to a height/weight/fat prior. The photos are segmented locally and normalized to 88 horizontal contour rows. For each angle and height, the projected outer and separated limb intervals of the mesh are compared with the observed silhouette runs. Visible edge correspondences drive regularized horizontal X/Z scale and translation estimates per region, smoothed across rows and blended at joints. Two fitting passes alter actual vertex coordinates. This is multi-view silhouette-constrained **template deformation**, not camera-calibrated photogrammetry, neural reconstruction, or arbitrary-pose 3D scanning. Vertical proportions are fixed to the template. The model cannot infer occluded surfaces, face likeness, internal tissues, sex-specific anatomy, skin texture, body-fat distribution or true muscle mass. It is stylized, not a photorealistic digital twin. No commercial scanning model or third-party ML weights are bundled; external model downloads were unavailable during development. See `BODY_MESH_LICENSE.md` for the original CC0 mesh.

Selected manual girths/widths then iteratively constrain the deformed surface, using template cross-section anchors. The solver is approximate, especially shoulder width/girth and interacting or conflicting constraints; inspect residuals instead of assuming exact fit. A regional shape correction can change the fitted dimensions. The engine reports pre-fit/photo-fit/final projected edge RMSE, manual dimensional RMSE, angle coverage and per-section residuals. It reports geometric fit, not measurement accuracy or resemblance. “Photo-assisted cm” estimates use the photo-fitted template and height/weight/fat priors before manual girth fitting. They are not independent tape or medical measurements. No clinical or real-person repeatability validation has been performed. Test metrics in this release use synthetic silhouettes, not actual ZOZOFIT scans.

## View, save and compare

Drag/pinch/scroll the model or use view controls. The measurement line selector projects a cross-section guide (not a depth-occluded tape rendering). Muscle regions are template surface labels. Choose a saved scan as a comparison, switch on overlay, blend or animate. Heatmap shows signed displacement along the comparison mesh's vertex normals (red outward, blue inward, saturates at ±3 cm); it is not a direct measure of tissue changes. Compare only similarly captured scans. Symmetry estimates are affected by pose and segmentation. Use the goal simulator to make hypothetical changes without overwriting the record. Export OBJ (cm) or binary glTF/GLB (m) of the current displayed model, or download an HTML report and print/save as PDF. Goal previews are not saved as actual scans. The older Body Studio retains saved measurement-only goals and timeline.

## Privacy and backups

Raw photos and video frames remain in browser memory during the session; discard the draft after saving or close the page. Saved records contain the fitted mesh, approved silhouette contours, input measurements, dates and derived metrics, not source photographs. These data are still sensitive. Local IndexedDB is not encrypted at rest and can be read by someone with the same browser profile. Encrypted scan backups use the existing AES-GCM/PBKDF2 backup code (256-bit AES, 600,000 PBKDF2 iterations, random salt and IV); scan backup passphrases cannot be recovered. Export scans separately in the scanner and existing workouts/photos separately in Settings. Restore merges records by ID with explicit confirmation; delete permanently removes a saved scan. Don't put backups in a public GitHub repository. Draft photos are not included in backups. Browser storage can be cleared or evicted, so keep private backups. Optional FitLog cloud sync still operates on the old data only. Runtime is dependency-free and all scan fitting is local after app assets are cached; real hosted offline/camera testing remains outstanding.

## Validation limitations and next steps

No MakeHuman/SMPL or learned landmark/segmentation asset was downloaded or integrated. No actual anatomical joint detection, automated turn recognition, neural background removal on complex scenes, true camera calibration, or validated equivalence to ZOZOFIT. These require licensed assets, a more robust capture/fitting pipeline, and independent real-person scan validation. The existing Body Studio remains available if photos cannot be segmented reliably. Do not use a scan for diagnosis, medical decisions or exact body composition. Full capture on a physical phone, video codec compatibility, native IndexedDB and service-worker installation need device testing; see `SCAN_TESTING.md`.
